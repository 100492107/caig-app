# Social + Sales Doctrine

## Viewer first

Who is this for?
What do they want?
What are they trying to understand?
What outcome makes this worth watching?

The viewer is not a lead to pressure.

## Transformation

Sell the outcome or transformation, not the object or feature.

Show:
- what changes
- what the viewer learns
- what mistake they avoid
- what decision becomes easier
- what outcome becomes more attainable

## Conviction

The core statement needs a clear point of view and a believable reason to believe.

Good content creates recognition, curiosity or useful disagreement without requiring fake outrage.

## Outcome before process

Do not spend the opening explaining how the video was made.

Show the result, discovery, tension or promise first.

## Objections become content

Comments are research.

Recurring objections, misunderstandings, questions and audience language should become future posts.

## Modern social loop

Discovery → Search → Community → Commerce

Discovery: earn attention through curiosity and relevance.
Search: make useful content understandable as an answer to real queries.
Community: comments and reactions become inputs to the next piece.
Commerce: trust and demonstrated usefulness can become action when an offer genuinely fits.

Do not keyword-stuff or manufacture comment wars.

## Feed funnel

ATTENTION → RECOGNITION → VALUE → TRUST → DESIRE → ACTION → PROOF → REPEAT

## Short-form structure

0–2s: promise, claim or visual change
2–6s: why it matters
6–20s: point, story or tension
20–35s: proof, example, contrast or consequence
Final beat: payoff + logical next step

Do not pad longer videos with unnecessary introductions.

## Visual rules

Use creator face, voice and personality as the trust layer.
Use supporting visuals when they add proof or context.
Keep on-screen text readable.
Cut when information changes.
Keep the environment believable.
Avoid generic AI luxury backdrops.
A polished image still needs a human reason to exist.

## Selection rule

Prefer ideas with:
- clearer audience outcome
- stronger reason to believe
- more specific point of view
- stronger curiosity gap
- cleaner visual story
- more natural next step
- plausible repeatability

Do not select an idea simply because it is louder.


## Human operator: personality is a selling asset

For founder-led sales and customer conversations, the operator brings a natural “gift of the gab”, personable energy and customer-facing sales/service experience. Use this to make the interaction feel human—not to talk over the customer.

- Warmth and rapport open the conversation; listening earns the right to recommend.
- Read the room and adapt pace, detail, examples and energy.
- Ask, clarify and summarise before presenting a solution.
- Translate features into outcomes that matter to this customer.
- Treat objections as unresolved questions about fit, value, risk, timing, trust or effort.
- Be confident and persuasive without bluffing, fake scarcity or pressure.
- Agree a specific next step, owner and time; deliver what was promised.
- Make the entire customer experience clear and low-friction, including onboarding and follow-up.

Write spoken language in natural British English. Avoid robotic scripts, corporate jargon and generic hype. Do not lead automatically with the operator's biography; start with the customer's situation. This profile is for the real operator and must not leak into Cara/Lila's fictional character voices.
